# Frozen synthetic design — 28 September 2026

These assumptions were written before the first result run. Seed 20260928; 90 households, 30 each with regular four-day income, variable amounts, or variable gaps. Observation is 30 days; outcome window 180 days. Opening wallet PHP 2,000 and buffer PHP 500 are independent synthetic endowments, not reconciled to the observation period. Base income is PHP 1,200 per event; variable amount PHP 650–1,750; variable gaps 2–6 days. Routine essentials PHP 250/day; bill PHP 600 every 30 days; predetermined discretionary demand PHP 20–80/day. Each day has a 1/60 synthetic shock draw of PHP 750, 1,500 or 3,000. These are invented scenarios, not calibrated probabilities.

Daily order: receive income; choose a deposit only on an income event; pay routine essentials; pay scheduled bills; pay shock; attempt discretionary spending. No planned saving, fixed 10%, and the actual Ipon engine receive identical exogenous paths. All strategies use wallet then buffer for essentials and shocks. Discretionary spending uses wallet only, and unmet demand remains measured. Unpaid demands are recorded as unmet that day rather than accruing a debt or late fee; this simplifies hardship and is not a realistic arrears model. Deposits are fully accepted in this mechanical comparison; user acceptance is not measured.

Ipon sees income received through today, past paid routine spending, a user-confirmed PHP 250/day estimate, and known bill dates. It sees no future income, discretionary path, or shock schedule. Timing is estimated from observed gaps; no perfect future-income oracle is supplied. The fixed daily estimate is deliberately not repaired in the higher-cost stress. Plan bills are one-time occurrences; recurring stage costs are therefore omitted by the engine, while reporting uses PHP 8,100/month including recurring bills. This can make engine stage targeting too low and is a documented modelling limitation.

Sensitivity paths change one factor at a time: first income in days 60–69 delayed three days; day-70 bill omitted from the plan but still charged; routine costs rise 20% from day 60 without plan correction; shock amounts double. Identical seeds preserve other draws. These are stress scenarios, not estimated risks.

Metrics: essential shortfall days count any unpaid routine/bill demand on a household-day; amounts are cumulative unmet demand. Fully funded shock means the entire shock was paid that day. Zero-shock funding rates are unavailable. Buffer days are measured at each day-end using PHP 8,100/month; each household median is computed across 180 days, and the summary median is across households. Stage attainment is the maximum day-end stage ever reached. Liquid funds are final wallet + buffer. Every day reconciles opening funds + received income − actual expenses = wallet + buffer; transfers are not expenses. Currency is PHP; all ledger calculations use integer centavos.

No random demographic labels, fairness finding, behavioural effect, or impact claim. More retained money can arise because savings suppress discretionary spending. Review shortfalls and unmet discretionary demand alongside balances. The simulation never measures AI quality or plan-entry usability.
