# Ipon Buffer: 10-minute presentation

November 4, 2026
Target pace: about 125 words per minute. Rehearse with a timer.

## 1. Mika knows her budget. She does not know local prices. (0:00–1:00)

Meet Mika, an illustrative customer rather than someone we have interviewed. She has two thousand pesos in her everyday wallet, groceries to buy, and an emergency fund she wants to build. Her difficulty is practical: she does not know which nearby shop offers a good price for the things she actually needs. Checking several stores costs time and transport money. Even when she finds something affordable, she does not know how much she can put aside without leaving herself short. Ipon Buffer connects those two decisions. It helps her learn from nearby shoppers, then review a contribution based on her essential expenses. The starting point is a useful shopping decision, with emergency savings as the longer-term purpose.

## 2. Ipon Buffer (1:00–2:00)

Ipon Buffer is a feature layer designed for an existing e-wallet business. The pitch is: Venmo makes payments social. Ipon Buffer makes local price knowledge social, and connects it to emergency savings. This is positioning, not a claim that we have outperformed Venmo. A wallet already has customers, payment habits, and merchant relationships. Ipon Buffer adds a specific reason to return between payments: finding affordable essentials nearby and helping neighbours do the same. The savings recommendation remains the core. The community makes that core relevant during an ordinary shopping trip. For the ASEAN Financial Health Challenge, the proposal links everyday spending decisions with the ability to build an emergency buffer, rather than treating savings as a separate chore.

## 3. One screen connects discovery and saving (2:00–3:00)

Here is the journey we can demonstrate. Mika logs in and sees her buffer banner above a photo-first feed. A report names the item, pack size, store, and observed price. She can open the post for its details, thank the contributor, or confirm the same price after checking it herself. She can also share her own finding with a photo. When she wants to save, she reviews her expenses and the app calculates a contribution. One tap confirms that displayed amount. The app then shows a completed deposit, rather than immediately asking for another smaller one. In the synthetic default example, a one-hundred-and-twenty-peso deposit moves money from the everyday wallet into the buffer. Price reports support discovery. They do not automatically change her budget or move money.

## 4. AI helps explain expenses (3:00–4:00)

The AI makes planning easier to use. Mika can describe an upcoming expense in ordinary language, such as a school cost or an expected bill. A local model proposes structured plan fields. She must review and confirm them before the plan changes. The AI does not control the wallet or decide transfers. The deterministic savings engine works with integer centavos, protects planned essentials, and validates the contribution. This separation matters commercially because a partner can explain which part assists the customer and which part enforces financial rules. The prototype supports a local model on the demo computer. It also keeps manual planning available. We should describe the AI as an assistance layer, with user review, rather than claiming it predicts every emergency or guarantees affordability.

## 5. A focused combination (4:00–5:00)

The innovation is the combination and its purpose. GasBuddy demonstrates community price reporting in fuel. Venmo already offers social recommendations and cashback, including recommendations backed by transactions. So it would be inaccurate to dismiss competitors as generic feeds or claim that social finance itself is new. Ipon Buffer focuses the experience on local essential purchases and connects that knowledge to an explicit emergency-savings workflow inside a wallet. The competitive argument is a tighter fit to this customer problem. We still need to test whether that focus delivers more useful visits and better savings outcomes. Today, we can demonstrate the joined-up prototype. We cannot claim superior retention, lower acquisition cost, or better financial health without a comparative pilot.

## 6. A useful action earns a social response (5:00–6:00)

The behaviour design starts with a real need. Mika wants to buy essentials, which gives her motivation. A short, visual feed and simple confirmation action reduce effort. Seeing a relevant price while she is already using the wallet provides a prompt. That maps to the Fogg behaviour model: motivation, ability, and a prompt must come together. After she contributes, appreciation and independent confirmations provide social recognition. Contribution points reward useful reporting, while buffer progress makes the financial goal visible. These are proposed mechanisms, not measured behaviour change. We would test whether recognition brings people back to make accurate contributions. We would not reward overspending, hide withdrawal controls, or equate likes with verified prices. Scheduled notifications are a future experiment, not something the current prototype already delivers.

## 7. Revenue fits the wallet’s existing business (6:00–7:00)

There are two direct commercial routes. First, a wallet operator can license the module and pay for integration and support. Second, merchants can fund targeted offers, with a clearly priced campaign service fee. The prototype demonstrates a simulated purchase, merchant cashback, and an explicit transfer of that cashback into the buffer. It does not collect real campaign fees. An illustrative campaign might fund ten pesos of cashback on a qualifying essentials purchase, with the fee agreed separately. Profit depends on that fee exceeding hosting, support, moderation, and fraud costs. Optional referrals to a partner savings or investment product could be explored later. We should not promise interest income from wallet balances. That depends on the partner’s actual products and arrangements, and emergency funds need accessible, appropriate treatment.

## 8. The partner buys measurable outcomes (7:00–8:00)

For a stakeholder, engagement matters only if it improves a business or customer outcome. We would measure whether users return for relevant prices, whether merchants receive additional qualifying purchases, and whether customers build buffers without leaving essentials uncovered. More time in a feed is not enough. The strongest commercial case would connect incremental merchant activity or retention to the cost of running the feature. A pilot should track fresh confirmed reports, repeat useful visits, completed deposits, net buffer growth after withdrawals, and campaign cost per incremental purchase. These are proposed measures, not current results. The partner can then compare a participating group with a suitable control group. That gives a purchasing decision a basis beyond downloads, likes, or an attractive demo.

## 9. A feature on top of the wallet (8:00–9:00)

The product is meant to sit on top of an existing wallet and its business structures. GCash or Maya are examples of potential customers, not current partners. The wallet would keep responsibility for identity, balances, payment authorisation, and merchant settlement. Ipon Buffer would supply the community experience and savings guidance through a defined integration. Today we have a reusable React module, backend APIs, and an Android WebView demo APK. That APK proves the mobile experience. It is not a binary another company can simply insert into its app. A production integration would adapt the host session, permissions, wallet events, and design. The commercial offer is therefore a modular product with integration work, rather than a separate consumer wallet competing for the same customers.

## 10. A neighbourhood pilot inside one wallet (9:00–10:00)

The first route to market is a focused pilot inside one wallet partner. Start with one community and a small cluster of merchants, so customers see enough relevant information on their first visit. Merchants and a small contributor group can seed accurate essentials prices. The wallet can expose the feature at an existing shopping or budgeting moment. Optional store signage can help customers discover that feature within the wallet they already use. This approach reduces the need to build an independent app audience, though it still requires partner approval and customer adoption. The pilot should prove report quality, useful repeat visits, and sustainable campaign economics before expansion. The ask is a wallet integration partner and a neighbourhood pilot. Ipon Buffer’s promise is practical: help people shop with better local knowledge and make an informed choice to strengthen their emergency buffer.
