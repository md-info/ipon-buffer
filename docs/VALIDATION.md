# Research and pilot proposal — not conducted

Recruit 8–10 prospective Philippine irregular-income workers for exploratory interviews using a partner-approved, voluntary recruitment process. This small sample explores comprehension and usability; it cannot estimate population impact. No findings, quotes or participant records exist yet.

Consent script: “We are testing a prototype with fictional money. Participation is voluntary. You can skip any question or stop. Please do not share account credentials or identifying financial details. We will ask you to complete planning tasks and explain what the screen means. Recording requires separate permission. We will agree what notes we retain, who can access them, and when they are deleted before starting. This does not affect any financial service you receive.” Obtain reviewed local wording before recruitment.

Interview/tasks:
1. Describe, without exact personal amounts, how you decide what an irregular payment must cover.
2. In a fictional plan, enter a bill, its date, and the next expected income. What information would you hesitate to disclose?
3. Explain “protected for essentials” in your own words. Does it guarantee affordability? Record the response before prompting.
4. Add a missing bill. Predict whether the suggestion should change, then explain the displayed result.
5. Delay expected income. What would you change before saving again?
6. Withdraw savings, then pay an emergency. Explain why these are different actions.
7. Revoke analysis consent and retrieve funds. Locate the retained-data explanation.
8. Identify whether a refill is scheduled automatically. Correct any misunderstanding and record it.

Counterbalance matched manual and AI entry tasks in AB/BA order, with different but equally complex expenses. Record task completion time, final confirmed amount/date/recurrence/inclusion errors, user corrections, clarification turns, abandonment, and understanding that AI drafts require review. Record order, language and assistance given. Separate extraction accuracy before review from confirmed-field accuracy afterward. Predefine error scoring and use a second reviewer on ambiguous cases. Report failures, denominators and withdrawals; avoid claiming statistical significance from exploratory sessions.

90-day pilot proposal: days 1–30 research, partner discovery and risk/integration review; days 31–60 shadow recommendations with no transfers; days 61–90 limited opt-in operation only after partner/legal/security approval. Assign partner owners for custody/access and reconciliation, researcher for consent/data handling, and engineering owner for incidents. Prototype authors cannot represent these approvals as obtained.

Predefine essential-shortfall days/amounts, accessible liquid buffer, withdrawal success and latency, comprehension, opt-in/decline rates and retention. Collect a baseline observation period and agree a comparison design before launch. Randomisation, sample size and numerical thresholds require feasibility and power work; they are not specified as achieved targets. Count dropouts and missing observations separately, report attrition by group, and retain intent-to-treat reporting if randomised. Do not count absence of data as no shortfall.

Stop/pause conditions: any unauthorised movement, impaired access to funds, reconciliation mismatch, data disclosure, or repeated material misunderstanding. Stop transfers, preserve access and audit evidence, notify responsible partner staff through agreed procedures, investigate and re-consent before restarting. Escalation owners and numerical monitoring thresholds must be agreed before a real pilot.
